Instrument types and how they work

7 types · a cheat sheet to keep them straight

1 · DEX perp on a stock category A

trade.xyz and all of HIP-3 · Lighter · Ostium · Vest · Aster · edgeX · GRVT · ApeX · gTrade · Extended · Helix
What it is
A synthetic perpetual: there is no stock, only a position against an order book / AMM pool, tied to the underlying price through an oracle (Pyth, Stork; Ostium uses a licensed Nasdaq feed, trade.xyz — S&P).
Whose liquidity
Native on-chain order book (HL, Lighter) or an AMM risk pool (Vest, gTrade, Ostium). This is the only category with an honest public order book 24/7.
Price anchoring
Funding every 1–8h pulls the perp toward the oracle; on weekends the oracle freezes at Friday close → the perp trades "Monday expectations" — hence the basis signal.
Hours
24/7 (Lighter still 24/5).
Risks
Oracle dependence (flash −45% on Ventuals), thin tails, funding dislocations when the crowd is one-sided.
What we monitor
OI, funding, depth@bps, basis vs NYSE, new listings. No history exists anywhere → our tick recorders.

2 · CEX perp on a stock category C · largest

Binance TradFi · OKX · Bitget · Gate · Bybit · MEXC · KuCoin · Coinbase INTX · Crypto.com · Kraken Deriv · BingX · (HTX)
What it is
The same synthetic perp, but on a CEX: USDT margin, 10–50x leverage, the exchange's central order book. No stock and no issuer behind the instrument.
Whose liquidity
Exchange market makers. Volumes are huge ($8.8B/24h), but vol/OI is extreme — flow is intraday, depth is more modest than volume.
Price anchoring
Index price with an oracle (often Pyth) + funding; outside NYSE hours some exchanges cap the index at ±3–5% from the anchor — weekend moves are clamped.
Hours
24/7.
Risks
Exchange rules (delistings without warning — the MEXC renaming case), ADL, negligible OI in the tail.
What we monitor
Same as above + funding divergence across exchanges (carry) and the listing template (similar everywhere — a new contract on one exchange often foreshadows listings on the rest).

3 · Tokenized stock, spot category B

issuers: Backed (xStocks) · Ondo GM · Dinari (dShares) · Binance bStocks — traded on Kraken/Bybit/Bitget/Gate/Gemini/… and on-chain
What it is
A 1:1 token on a real share: the issuer buys the share through a broker, holds it with a custodian (SPV in Switzerland/Jersey/ADGM) and mints the token. This is the only spot instrument with a real share behind it.
Whose liquidity
The issuer's market makers (Flow Traders for Dinari) + mint/redeem arbitrageurs. During NYSE hours the token is arbitraged against the real share → tight spread; overnight the MM quotes "on expectations" → wider spread, lower depth.
Dividends / rights
Dividends are reinvested into the token or paid out (Bitget Stocks 2.0); no voting rights.
Hours
24/7 (some Kraken pairs — 24/5).
Risks
Issuer risk (SPV, jurisdiction), redemption mechanics limited to institutions, thin books ($60k–$1.2M volume per pair).
What we monitor
Spread/depth per venue, token basis vs NYSE, growth in the number of issued tickers (Backed's target — 500).

4 · AMM pool on a tokenized stock category E

Raydium · Jupiter · PancakeSwap · Uniswap · Felix · HyperCore (order-book spot)
What it is
The same tokens as type 3, but in DEX pools: liquidity = pool TVL, price = the x*y=k curve (or CLMM ranges).
Whose liquidity
LP providers; depth is set by TVL, not an order book — we compute depth@bps from the pool curve.
Hours
24/7, executes on-chain — the leg for on-chain strategies (first block after news, etc.).
Risks
IL for LPs, thin pools, MEV/sandwiching on entry.
What we monitor
TVL, volume, price vs CEX (divergence = arbitrage signal), new pools (linked to the listing×on-chain sheet).

5 · Pre-IPO: perps and SPV tokens category D

perps: Binance · INTX · OKX · Kraken · Ventuals · Helix · Crypto.com — spot: PreStocks · Jarsy · Dinari SPCXD
What it is
Two different animals. Perps — pure synthetics on a private company; there is no underlying price, quotes are an implied valuation ($B on Ventuals) or $/share (CEX) derived from secondary deals/rounds. SPV tokens — a token on a stake in an SPV that (supposedly) holds the shares.
Whose liquidity
Perps — exchange MMs, no oracle or a subjective one → funding and settlement rules decide everything. Tokens — Jupiter/Meteora pools, very thin.
Risks
Perps: settlement to the IPO price under venue rules, flash moments (Ventuals −45%). Tokens: the company can declare SPV transfers void (OpenAI/Anthropic, −40% in May) — the token is not a claim on the share.
What we monitor
Cross-venue OI/vol, convergence to IPO (the SPCX case today), perp price divergence across venues, new names.

6 · CFD via MT5 out of scope · opt. recorder

Bybit TradFi (~150 names, Infra Capital, Mauritius) · Gate CFD (44 pairs)
What it is
A contract-for-difference against a broker: no share and no exchange order book — a single counterparty (the exchange's LP), MetaTrader 5 platform.
Whose liquidity
An undisclosed LP; Bybit's $400k/account cap on stock CFDs hints at the depth. Dividends handled via position adjustments.
Data
NOT visible in exchange APIs. Channel: MT5 demo account (free) → the MetaTrader5 package (Python, Windows-only) → ticks via copy_ticks_from; DOM not guaranteed. A separate mini-project.
Why watch it
The only feed of CFD liquidity — prices differ from the perps; a potential reference in off-hours.

7 · Brokerage of real stocks out of scope · no API

Binance Stocks (~7,000, Nest→Alpaca) · Crypto.com Stocks (US-only, Foris→Apex) · MEXC RealStocks (Atomic Vaults) · Robinhood/Webull 24h
What it is
A regular brokerage account wrapped in a crypto app: the order goes to an introducing broker → clearing firm → PFOF wholesaler or exchange. Price = US market NBBO.
Whose liquidity
The US market itself. Overnight everyone shares a single leg — Blue Ocean ATS (20:00–04:00 ET, limit-only, thin) → brokerage creates no independent prices overnight; off-hours price discovery is done by the perps (Binance NVDA perp $99M/day vs $0.6M on bStocks).
Access
Exchange KYC + broker agreement; Binance/MEXC/Bybit — not for US persons, Crypto.com — US only. Thousands of tickers, but nothing to poll — no one exposes an API.
Why it is in the monitor
Only as a reference for "where the long tail is accessible" + a source of future tokenized-version listings.
